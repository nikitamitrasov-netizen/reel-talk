The format

A session runs on rails

Ninety minutes, four moves. The site carries the timing, the questions and the handouts so the teacher can watch the film too.

  1. 1

    Watch together

    A room code and a shared clock. Everyone starts at the same second, pauses together, and sees the same cue land at 00:42:10.

    Open the player →
  2. 2

    Talk it through

    Eight questions per film, ordered from “what happened” to “what would you have done”. A question deck keeps the room in one conversation.

    Play the scene

    Gap-fills, quote matching and role-play cards built on the film’s own language — plus scenes to act out in class.

    Small print

    Where the films come from

    Reel Talk does not host films. Every title links out to a legal source — a streaming service found through JustWatch, an official trailer, or the Internet Archive for public-domain features. The three open-screen titles are public domain and stream from the Archive’s own servers.

    Where a film is not available in your country, the club still works: the watch room supplies the shared clock, and everyone plays their own licensed copy. That is what the clock is for.

    Availability and prices are set by the platforms, not by us. Film titles, years and credits are used for identification only.
